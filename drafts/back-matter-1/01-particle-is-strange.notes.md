# Entry 1 — The Particle Is Strange. This Is Now Its Name.

Status: approved concept; full draft pending

## Scope

Human scientific names frequently preserve first impressions and metaphors. The entry should use the six quark flavors—up, down, strange, charm, top, and bottom—then compound Leth's confusion with flavor, color charge, and spin. These words are functional scientific terms even though none describes the ordinary sensory property it appears to name.

Historical calibration: strangeness was named for the unexpected production and decay behavior of particles; the later strange-quark designation inherited the terminology. Do not present “huh, that’s strange” as a documented quotation. It is the correct comic compression of the intellectual process.

## Approved core passage

> I requested the formal designations.
>
> The physicist repeated the same words.
>
> Up. Down. Strange. Charm. Top. Bottom.
>
> I clarified that I required the scientific terminology rather than the mnemonic provided to children.
>
> The physicist became offended.
>
> These were the scientific terms.
>
> Humanity had penetrated the fundamental structure of matter and discovered that reality was composed, in part, of direction, social appeal, and a general sense that something was not behaving normally.

## Approved ending concept

> This is how young scientific civilizations name the universe.
>
> First they point.
>
> Then they react.
>
> The paperwork arrives later.
