# Entry 8 — The Species Has Acquired a Personality.

Status: approved concept; full draft pending

## Scope

Humans assign personalities not only to individual animals, but to entire species. A species becomes a cultural character: capybaras are calm and universally accepting; kangaroos are muscular and confrontational; geese are malicious; raccoons are criminals; dolphins are suspiciously intelligent; golden retrievers are well-intentioned fools; cats are aristocrats or domestic tyrants.

These reputations contain fragments of observed behavior, but humans expand those fragments into temperament, motive, and social role.

## Approved core passage

> Humans assign collective personalities to other species.
>
> The capybara is considered peaceful.
>
> The kangaroo is considered confrontational.
>
> The goose is considered malicious.
>
> The raccoon is considered a thief.
>
> These classifications do not appear in formal zoology.
>
> They are nevertheless communicated with greater confidence than much of the formal zoology.

## Australia

Humans collectively emotionify Australia as a conscious ecological adversary.

> Humans frequently state that everything in Australia is attempting to kill them.
>
> This is inaccurate.
>
> Many Australian organisms are indifferent to humans.
>
> Several are harmless.
>
> Some are merely equipped to kill a human should the relationship deteriorate.
>
> Humans have interpreted this collective capacity as intent and assigned the entire continent a hostile personality.
>
> Australia has become, in human culture, not a location but an adversary.

## Capybaras

> Capybaras have acquired the cultural role of patient interspecies diplomats.
>
> Humans display images of capybaras sitting near birds, monkeys, turtles, and other animals as evidence that the capybara has achieved a form of social wisdom.
>
> It is possible that the animal is simply sitting.
>
> Humans have elected to interpret the sitting as a philosophy.

Classification: **Species Observation → Collective Characterization → Cultural Personality**
