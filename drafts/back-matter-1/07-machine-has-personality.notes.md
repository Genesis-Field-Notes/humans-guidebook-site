# Entry 7 — The Machine Has Developed a Personality.

Status: full draft approved; preserved as `07-machine-has-developed-a-personality.md`

## Primary field specimen: Black Betty

A human named Jake acquired a maroon Dodge truck and named it **Black Betty**. The vehicle is not black. He subsequently welded a massive black angular steel bumper onto its front. The bumper contains a central light bar and embedded side lights. Combined with the existing grille and oversized tires, the modification gives the vehicle a face, a jaw, and the appearance of having been assigned gatekeeping responsibilities.

The human has a prior history involving vehicles and trees. The modification may constitute armor, deterrence, or formal notice to the local vegetation.

Reference photograph: `references/black-betty-front.jpeg`

## Governing behavior

Humans do not name every machine. They name machines that cross an invisible threshold into characterhood. Perceived personality may arise from appearance, reliability, unreliability, unusual noises, accumulated repairs, customization, shared history, or repeated refusal to perform one specific task under predictable conditions.

Humans do not merely detect this personality. They collaboratively construct it:

1. The human names the machine.
2. The name influences how the human interprets the machine.
3. The human modifies the machine to express the name.
4. The machine appears to possess the personality originally perceived.
