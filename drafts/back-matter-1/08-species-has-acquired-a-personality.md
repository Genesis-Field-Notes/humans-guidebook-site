# NOMENCLATURE ENTRY 8
## The Species Has Acquired a Personality.

**Subjects observed:** Entire animal species  
**Number of individuals consulted:** Insufficient  
**Collective character assigned:** Extensive  
**Scientific validity:** Variable  
**Cultural certainty:** Absolute

Humans assign collective personalities to other species.

The capybara is considered peaceful.

The kangaroo is considered confrontational.

The goose is considered malicious.

The raccoon is considered a thief.

The dolphin is considered intelligent in a manner humans find increasingly suspicious.

The golden retriever is considered kind, loyal, and unlikely to understand the full situation.

The cat is considered either an aristocrat, a domestic tyrant, or a small supernatural entity temporarily accepting food from humans.

These classifications do not appear in formal zoology.

They are nevertheless communicated with greater confidence than much of the formal zoology.

### From Behavior to Character

Every species exhibits characteristic behavior.

This is unremarkable.

Organisms possess different feeding strategies, social structures, defensive responses, reproductive behaviors, patterns of movement, and relationships with their environments.

Humans observe these patterns.

They then add motive.

A raccoon does not merely manipulate a container to obtain food.

It is committing a burglary.

A goose does not merely defend territory.

It has selected violence.

A capybara does not merely tolerate the proximity of another animal.

It has achieved inner peace.

The transition occurs rapidly.

First, the human observes what the animal does.

Then the human explains what kind of person would do it.

The animal becomes a character.

### The Capybara

Capybaras have acquired the cultural role of patient interspecies diplomats.

Humans circulate images of capybaras sitting near birds, monkeys, turtles, ducks, dogs, cats, and other animals as evidence that the capybara has achieved a form of social wisdom.

It is possible that the animal is simply sitting.

Humans have elected to interpret the sitting as a philosophy.

The capybara’s large body, still posture, rounded face, and apparent tolerance of surrounding activity produce an impression of complete emotional stability.

Other animals climb onto it. The capybara remains.

Birds stand on it. The capybara remains.

A human places an object on its head. The capybara remains.

The animal has become an internet-era spiritual advisor whose primary teaching is remaining partially submerged in warm water while refusing to react.

No capybara has published a philosophy of universal acceptance.

The humans have written one on its behalf.

### The Goose

The goose occupies the opposite role.

Geese are considered malicious.

A goose approaching a human with its neck extended is not described as displaying defensive or territorial behavior.

It is described as **starting something.**

Humans speak of geese as though the species participates in a coordinated campaign of intimidation.

The goose blocks paths. The goose occupies parks. The goose hisses. The goose pursues.

The goose appears aware that many humans are reluctant to fight an animal in public.

The human encounters the goose expecting a bird.

The goose responds as local enforcement.

The relationship deteriorates immediately.

### The Raccoon

The raccoon is considered a criminal.

The raccoon possesses dark facial markings resembling the mask humans assign to thieves in simplified illustrations.

This is an unfortunate coincidence.

The animal also possesses dexterous front paws, curiosity, adaptability, and a strong interest in food containers.

This is a more unfortunate coincidence.

A raccoon opening a bin is not described as foraging. It is conducting a heist.

A raccoon entering a structure is not lost. It has broken in.

Humans have developed an entire criminal identity for an animal whose principal offense is competence near poorly secured trash.

The mask does considerable narrative work.

### The Golden Retriever

The golden retriever is considered morally good.

Not well behaved.

Not intelligent.

Good.

The same animal may consume an object that is not food, become trapped behind furniture it walked behind voluntarily, fall into water while attempting to inspect the water, and greet a dangerous stranger as though the stranger has returned from war.

These events do not damage the reputation. They strengthen it.

The golden retriever is not expected to exercise judgment.

It is expected to mean well.

The animal has become the cultural embodiment of goodness without strategy.

### The Cat

Cats have acquired several overlapping personalities.

They are aristocrats.

They are tyrants.

They are assassins.

They are small domestic gods whose worshippers are required to clean a box.

A cat may knock an object from a surface while looking directly at the human who placed it there.

The human does not describe this as ordinary exploratory behavior.

The human says:

> He knows what he did.

Intent has entered the room.

### Dolphins

Dolphins were initially assigned the role of cheerful marine intellectual.

Continued observation complicated this reputation.

They remain intelligent.

The humans are now less certain that the intelligence is reassuring.

This has produced a distinctive cultural judgment: dolphins know considerably more than they are saying and may not be using the information responsibly.

### Kangaroos

Kangaroos are interpreted as confrontational.

Their upright posture, muscular frame, large feet, and habit of facing an observer directly create the impression of an animal waiting for a disagreement to become physical.

A human shown an image of a large kangaroo may say:

> He looks like he wants to fight me.

The kangaroo has not met this human.

Distance has not prevented the assignment of motive.

### Australia

Humans frequently state that everything in Australia is attempting to kill them.

This is inaccurate.

Many Australian organisms are indifferent to humans.

Several are harmless.

Some are merely equipped to kill a human should the relationship deteriorate.

Humans have interpreted this collective capacity as intent and assigned the entire continent a hostile personality.

Australia has become, in human culture, not a location but an adversary.

### Australian Naming Conventions

Australian common names frequently prioritize operational clarity.

The red-bellied black snake is black and has a red belly.

The eastern brown snake is brown and found in the east.

The saltwater crocodile is a crocodile frequently encountered in saltwater.

The common death adder is the death adder the humans apparently expect you to encounter most routinely.

At some point, taxonomic creativity was judged less important than communicating the nature and approximate location of the emergency.

The naming system had ceased attempting to comfort anyone.

### Collective Intent

Humans often discuss these reputations as though every member of the species has agreed to uphold them.

> Geese are mean.

> Raccoons are thieves.

> Capybaras are friends with everyone.

The statement concerns an entire species.

The evidence frequently concerns one animal, one video, or one encounter near an unsecured container.

### Reputation Before Encounter

Humans can meet the cultural personality before meeting the animal.

The reputation changes what they notice.

A calm capybara confirms serenity.

An aggressive goose confirms malice.

A raccoon near a lid confirms criminal planning.

The animal enters an established role and its behavior is edited accordingly.

### Cultural Employment

Once a species acquires a personality, humans employ it symbolically.

Capybaras represent peace.

Golden retrievers represent uncomplicated goodness.

Raccoons represent charming disorder.

Geese represent hostility with municipal access.

The animal becomes shorthand for a human condition.

### The Animal’s Contribution

These reputations are not created from nothing.

The animal supplies posture, behavior, anatomy, sound, and repeated patterns.

The human then enlarges the observed feature until it becomes motive, temperament, ethics, and social role.

The process resembles the naming of machines.

A machine makes one recurring noise and becomes anxious.

A truck receives a name and grows into it.

An animal displays one memorable behavior and its entire species acquires character.

Reality begins the sentence.

The human finishes it.

### Field Note

I attempted to correct several of these descriptions during conversation.

I explained that the goose was likely defending territory.

The human agreed and continued calling it malicious.

I explained that the raccoon was seeking food rather than acquiring property through unlawful means.

The human agreed and referred to it as a repeat offender.

I explained that the capybara’s tolerance did not establish a coherent philosophy of peace.

The human displayed another image of a capybara sitting beside a duck.

The discussion ended there.

The human evidence was not scientific.

It was, however, visually persuasive.

### Final Observation

Humans emotionify reality.

They do not reserve this behavior for other humans.

They leave little sparks of themselves attached to animals, machines, plants, landscapes, planets, particles, and anything else encountered long enough to produce a reaction.

The capybara does not possess human serenity.

The human encountered its stillness and left serenity there.

The goose does not possess human malice.

The human encountered its aggression and left malice there.

The raccoon does not possess criminal intent.

The human encountered its dexterity beside an opened container and left theft there.

The kangaroo does not challenge distant viewers to combat.

The human encountered its posture and left confrontation there.

The object does not possess the emotion.

The name—or, in this case, the reputation—preserves the human who felt it.

Human language is covered in these emotional fossils.

Some preserve fear. Some preserve affection. Some preserve irritation. Some preserve wonder.

Some preserve the exact instant in which a human saw a capybara sitting calmly among several unrelated animals and decided that the creature understood coexistence better than humanity did.

The judgment is biologically indefensible.

It may still contain a truth about humans.

They recognize themselves everywhere.

Then they leave a description behind.

**GENERAL RULE:**

When humans observe a species long enough to recognize a behavioral pattern, they convert the pattern into character.

The animal supplies the behavior.

The human supplies the motive.
