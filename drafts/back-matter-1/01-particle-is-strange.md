# NOMENCLATURE ENTRY 1

## The Particle Is Strange. This Is Now Its Name.

**Discipline observed:** Particle physics  
**Naming authority:** Several generations of human scientists  
**Objects under discussion:** The fundamental constituents of matter  
**Expected terminology:** Formally descriptive  
**Terminology encountered:** Up, down, strange, charm, top, bottom

Humans have identified six varieties of elementary particle known as quarks.

This is a considerable scientific achievement.

Quarks are among the fundamental constituents of matter. They combine to produce protons, neutrons, and many other particles. Their behavior is governed by interactions so far removed from ordinary human experience that understanding them required generations of observation, mathematical invention, experimental engineering, and international cooperation.

The humans named the six varieties:

**Up.**

**Down.**

**Strange.**

**Charm.**

**Top.**

**Bottom.**

I requested the formal designations.

The physicist repeated the same words.

I clarified that I required the scientific terminology rather than the mnemonic provided to children.

The physicist became offended.

These were the scientific terms.

Humanity had penetrated the fundamental structure of matter and discovered that reality was composed, in part, of direction, social appeal, and a general sense that something was not behaving normally.

### Flavor

The six varieties of quark are collectively described as **flavors**.

They possess no flavor.

This clarification was apparently considered necessary only after I asked.

Nothing about a quark can be tasted. Attempting to taste one would not improve the experiment. Quarks do not occur independently under ordinary conditions, and the human sensory system is not equipped to distinguish elementary particles through culinary analysis.

Nevertheless, human physicists speak confidently of quark flavor.

This produces sentences in which strange, charm, top, and bottom are flavors, while red, green, and blue are colors, although none of these words refers to the property it ordinarily describes.

The scientists understand one another.

This is considered sufficient.

### Up and Down

The first pair are called **up** and **down**.

These particles are not positioned above or below one another.

They do not rise.

They do not descend.

An up quark placed beneath a down quark does not become incorrect.

The designations refer to relationships within a mathematical classification rather than physical direction. This is defensible.

It is also how humanity reached the foundation of ordinary matter and installed two elevator buttons.

Protons contain two up quarks and one down quark.

Neutrons contain one up quark and two down quarks.

The difference between much of the visible matter encountered by humans can therefore be expressed by rearranging the words *up* and *down.*

This explanation is scientifically useful.

It also sounds like instructions being given to someone attempting to move a sofa through a narrow doorway.

### Strange

The next designation emerged after humans encountered particles whose behavior did not match their expectations.

The particles were produced through one kind of interaction but decayed according to another, causing them to persist considerably longer than the humans anticipated.

The humans investigated.

They compared results.

They developed new mathematical descriptions.

They identified a previously unaccounted-for property.

They called the property **strangeness**.

Later, the relevant quark became the **strange quark**.

The particle itself had not changed.

The humans had been surprised by it.

The surprise became terminology.

This is an unusually pure example of emotional residue in scientific language. The name does not merely distinguish the particle. It preserves the moment when matter behaved differently from the way humans believed matter ought to behave.

Something unexpected occurred.

A human reaction followed.

The reaction was entered into the permanent record.

### Charm

Another quark is called **charm**.

This particle has not demonstrated charisma.

It does not attract other particles through social grace.

It has never made a favorable first impression at a gathering.

The charm quark participates in physical interactions of considerable importance. None of these interactions requires it to be charming in the ordinary human sense.

The designation nevertheless carries delight.

Where *strange* preserves the discomfort of an unexpected result, *charm* preserves pleasure: symmetry restored, a theoretical structure made more satisfying, a discovery welcomed rather than resisted.

One particle unsettled the humans.

Another pleased them.

The emotional distinction remains inside the names.

I find this scientifically irrelevant.

I have not found it meaningless.

### Top and Bottom

The final pair are called **top** and **bottom**.

They were also once discussed as **truth** and **beauty**.

This indicates that the naming process briefly passed through a philosophical phase before returning to furniture assembly.

The terms top and bottom do not establish permanent vertical positions. A top quark is not required to remain above a bottom quark. Orientation does not alter identity. Rotating the experimental apparatus will not reverse the classification.

The names are relational labels whose usefulness depends entirely upon humans agreeing not to interpret them as ordinary words while continuing to use the ordinary words.

This agreement appears throughout particle physics.

Human scientists employ familiar language.

They remove its familiar meaning.

They assign it a precise new meaning.

They then express surprise when everyone else becomes confused.

### Color

Quarks also possess a property called **color charge**.

The designated colors are commonly represented as red, green, and blue.

Quarks are not red, green, or blue.

Visible color is not involved.

The terminology describes a system of relationships within the strong interaction. The color labels help humans reason about combinations and balance using a familiar conceptual structure.

This is effective.

It is also evidence that physicists, having already consumed direction, personality, anatomy, flavor, truth, and beauty, proceeded into the visual arts.

The human language was not designed to describe phenomena at this scale.

The humans adapted it.

They took words built for bodies, meals, social judgments, and visible light and sent them into the atom carrying new instructions.

The words returned altered.

### Spin

Particles also possess **spin**.

They do not spin in the ordinary sense.

Early explanations often encourage humans to imagine a small object rotating around an axis. More advanced explanations then inform them that this image is wrong.

This is a recurring human educational method.

First, construct a useful misunderstanding.

Then refine it until it becomes knowledge.

Spin is an intrinsic quantum property with measurable consequences. The technical definition is precise. The ordinary word remains because it once gave the human mind somewhere to begin.

This may be the actual function of many human scientific names.

They are not always destinations.

They are handholds.

### The Metaphor Problem

Human science attempts to describe realities that evolved without regard for human language.

Every available word was originally developed for something else.

*Up* belonged to orientation.

*Flavor* belonged to food.

*Color* belonged to light.

*Charm* belonged to social experience.

*Strange* belonged to the feeling produced when reality failed to behave as expected.

The physicists borrowed them.

Once borrowed, the words acquired exact definitions. Equations constrained them. Experiments refined them. Entire scientific communities agreed upon how they would be used.

The terminology became precise.

The metaphor remained visible underneath.

My own civilization avoids much of this ambiguity through relational designations. A particle’s formal identifier communicates its measurable properties, interactions, symmetries, and position within the relevant system.

This is more efficient.

A student does not have to be told that the equivalent of *color* is not color, *spin* is not spinning, and *flavor* should not be tasted.

Nothing in the designation remembers the confusion that preceded understanding.

This was considered an advantage.

The human system preserves the scaffolding.

It shows where an ordinary mind first found a grip on an extraordinary thing.

### Field Note

Humans are capable of extraordinary precision.

They are also capable of placing that precision inside a word selected because someone, at some point, found the situation peculiar.

This should not be mistaken for intellectual carelessness.

The equations are careful.

The instruments are careful.

The definitions are careful.

The name may still amount to:

**That one is strange.**

The phrase becomes a label.

The label becomes a quantity.

The quantity enters the mathematics.

Generations later, another human studies the strange quark without knowing the person whose surprise remains attached to it.

The observer has disappeared.

The reaction survives.

### Final Observation

Humanity reached beneath the atom and found a world inaccessible to ordinary sight, touch, scale, and intuition.

They could not bring that world directly into human experience.

They did the next available thing.

They carried human experience into it.

Direction entered as up and down.

Taste entered as flavor.

Vision entered as color.

Motion entered as spin.

Unease entered as strange.

Delight entered as charm.

Human beings did not merely classify the particles.

They left small traces of themselves among them.

This is how young scientific civilizations name the universe.

First they point.

Then they react.

The paperwork arrives later.

---

**GENERAL RULE:**

When reality exceeds the human vocabulary, humans do not remain silent.

They assign an existing word additional responsibilities.
