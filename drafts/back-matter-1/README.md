# Back Matter I — After Contact

Status: unpublished working section

## Section title

**After Contact: The Human Spark in the Names of Things**

## Governing idea

Humans leave an emotional residue on nonhuman things through naming. A name can preserve not only what a thing is, but what happened inside the human observer when the encounter occurred. The thing remains nonhuman; the name retains a small spark of the human life that met it.

Working classification:

**Human Contact Behaviors → Affective Imprinting → Nomenclature**

## Approved entry sequence

1. **The Particle Is Strange. This Is Now Its Name.**  
   Scientific naming formalizes surprise, metaphor, and first impressions.
2. **The Program Is Called What It Does.**  
   Young technological societies name tools after the tasks and physical objects they replaced.
3. **The First Specimen Is “Common.”**  
   Discovery order disguises itself as objective classification.
4. **The Name Is Wrong. It Has Seniority.**  
   Familiar names and categories survive factual correction. Pluto must be a primary example: humans experienced its reclassification not as revised taxonomy, but as a demotion inflicted upon a beloved small planet.
5. **It Resembled a Horse from One Direction.**  
   New organisms are described through increasingly questionable resemblance.
6. **Humans Name Things After Humans.**  
   Eponyms turn discovery, prestige, affection, power, and memory into linguistic territory.
7. **The Machine Has Developed a Personality.**  
   Humans name machines after appearance, quirks, unreliability, shared history, and perceived temperament.

## Voice and emotional arc

Steward Leth begins with concern about imprecise human classification. His observations remain dry, formal, and funny, but gradually become fond. His own civilization's designations are more precise and contain more information about the thing. Human names contain more of the people who encountered it. By the end, he is no longer certain the human residue should be removed.

## Approved recurring formulations

- Human nomenclature is a record of where reality touched humanity.
- Humans emotionify reality.
- The object does not possess the emotion. The name preserves the human who felt it.
- The observer disappears. The reaction survives.
- Human language is covered in emotional fossils.
- First they point. Then they react. The paperwork arrives later.
- Humans touch the universe with language and leave fingerprints everywhere.
