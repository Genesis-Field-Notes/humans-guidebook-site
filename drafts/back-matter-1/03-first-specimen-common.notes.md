# Entry 3 — The First Specimen Is “Common.”

Status: approved concept; full draft pending

## Scope

Human taxonomy often records discovery order disguised as ontology. The first-known or locally familiar specimen receives the unmarked or “common” name. Every later relative must carry the burden of clarification through adjectives such as lesser, greater, false, true, eastern, western, red-bellied, long-tailed, short-tailed, giant, and pygmy.

## Approved core passage

> The “common” specimen is not necessarily the most numerous, representative, or structurally fundamental.
>
> It is frequently the one the humans named before realizing they would later require additional names.
>
> The second specimen must therefore carry the entire burden of clarification.
>
> This is how one animal becomes *the owl* and another becomes *the lesser mottled eastern marsh owl.*
>
> The second animal did nothing to deserve this.

Classification: **Taxonomic Development → Discovery Order → Adjectival Burden**
